## 1. Problem

People who work out at home have a big TV in the room but use it only to play videos. Nothing tells them when their form breaks down, so they risk injury and stop making progress. The products that do give live form feedback need dedicated hardware: Forme Life costs $2,495 plus $49/mo, and Peloton Guide costs $195 plus $24/mo. Free options like YouTube give no feedback at all.

## 2. Product

**FormVision turns a camera-equipped Fire TV into a form coach.** Pose tracking runs on the device and draws a skeleton over the live camera feed. When the user's form breaks, FormVision speaks a short, specific correction, generated by AWS Bedrock from joint angles only. Video never leaves the device.

**Hero moment (the demo video is built around this):** the user holds a plank. On the left of the screen is their live feed with a skeleton overlay; on the right is the target form. Their hips start to sag, and the hip segment of the skeleton turns to the fault state. A voice says *"Hips up a couple of inches — squeeze your glutes."* The user corrects, the skeleton returns to the good state, and a chime plays.

## 3. Goals and non-goals

### Goals (hackathon)
1. **G1:** A live demo on Fire OS (Android TV Emulator fed by the laptop webcam) with a skeleton overlay running at ≥ 15 fps.
2. **G2:** Accurate detection and spoken correction of plank faults (hip sag, hip pike), with the user hearing the correction ≤ 1.5 s after the fault starts.
3. **G3:** A 10-foot UI that is fully usable with the D-pad alone.
4. **G4:** Zero video or image data leaves the device. This is verifiable from the code and the network payloads.
5. **G5:** A submission package that is complete for the Fire TV track, plus the AWS Builder and Open Source mini challenges. It includes a friction log to earn the bonus of up to 10%.

### Non-goals (this build)
- Multiple users, accounts, profiles, workout history, cloud sync.
- Payments or subscription tiers. The freemium model is described only in the pitch.
- Rep counting for dynamic movements. Squat is a stretch goal only (§7).
- Phone-as-camera bridge for Fire TV Sticks (BYOC). Pitch only.
- Alexa+ skill or MCP integration.
- Medical or injury-diagnosis claims of any kind.

## 6. Functional requirements

### 6.1 Camera and tracking
- **FR-1** The app enumerates the available camera sources (built-in, USB UVC) and selects one by default.
- **FR-2** The app runs pose estimation on the device and emits landmarks (x, y, z, visibility) per frame to the UI layer.
- **FR-3** The app smooths landmarks over time to suppress jitter (see TECH-SPEC §5.2).
- **FR-4** The app provides a **Recorded source**: it replays a bundled landmark recording (JSON) through the same pipeline. It is clearly labelled "Replay" in the UI. It is used for the simulator, tests, and the demo backup.
- **FR-5** If the required landmarks have visibility < 0.5 for > 1 s, tracking is "lost". The UI prompts the user to step back into frame and pauses fault evaluation.

### 6.2 Setup / calibration
- **FR-6** Before a session starts, the app checks that the full body is in frame (shoulders, hips, knees, ankles visible) and that the user is side-on to the camera (required for plank).
- **FR-7** The app shows a live framing guide and a "Hold still — starting in 3…2…1" countdown once the checks pass for 1.5 s continuously.

### 6.3 Session
- **FR-8** Split-screen layout: live feed + overlay (left, ~60%), target form + cue text + timer (right, ~40%). See SITEMAP S-05.
- **FR-9** The plank rule engine evaluates the body-line angle (shoulder–hip–ankle) and classifies each frame as `GOOD`, `HIP_SAG`, `HIP_PIKE`, or `UNKNOWN`. Thresholds are in TECH-SPEC §6.
- **FR-10** A fault is **confirmed** only after it persists ≥ 600 ms (debounce). A fix is confirmed after `GOOD` persists ≥ 500 ms.
- **FR-11** When a fault is confirmed:
  - The skeleton segments involved switch to the fault style immediately.
  - A cue request is sent with numeric features only.
  - The spoken cue plays as soon as it is available.
- **FR-12** The cue cooldown is ≥ 4 s between spoken cues. The same fault is not repeated within 8 s unless it got worse by ≥ 10°.
- **FR-13** **Instant fallback cue:** if the Bedrock cue has not arrived within 1.2 s, the app plays a pre-rendered local cue for that fault. The late response is then dropped and never played over the fallback.
- **FR-14** Confirmed fixes trigger the success chime and switch the skeleton back to the good style.
- **FR-15** Pause/resume with the D-pad Select or Play/Pause key. Back opens the "End session?" confirmation.
- **FR-16** The session timer counts time held in plank. Time is not counted while tracking is lost.

### 6.4 Results
- **FR-17** The summary shows total time, % of time in `GOOD`, fault counts by type, and a one-line takeaway generated from the session aggregate. If Bedrock is unavailable, a local template is used instead.
- **FR-18** The summary offers "Try again" (primary focus) and "Home".

### 6.5 Settings and privacy
- **FR-19** Settings: camera source (Live / Replay), voice coach on/off, voice volume, skeleton overlay on/off, show debug HUD (fps, angle values).
- **FR-20** The privacy screen states plainly what is processed on the device and what is sent (the exact payload fields).

## 8. Non-functional requirements

| ID | Requirement | Target |
|---|---|---|
| NFR-1 | Pose inference rate on target device | ≥ 15 fps (goal 24+) |
| NFR-2 | UI frame rate during session | ≥ 30 fps; overlay must not block the JS thread |
| NFR-3 | Fault onset → audible cue (p90) | ≤ 1.5 s (Bedrock path), ≤ 0.8 s (local fallback) |
| NFR-4 | Privacy | No frames, images, or video sent anywhere. Payloads are numbers and enums only |
| NFR-5 | Cloud credentials | No long-lived AWS keys in the app binary (see TECH-SPEC §8) |
| NFR-6 | Offline behaviour | The app works fully without network; cues fall back to local audio |
| NFR-7 | 10-foot legibility | Readable at 3 m on a 55" screen; see BRANDING §5 |
| NFR-8 | Cost | Total AWS spend during the hackathon < $20 |
| NFR-9 | Safety copy | No medical claims; a one-line disclaimer on first launch |

## 9. Success metrics

**Hackathon (judging criteria):**

| Criterion | What earns a 5 | Evidence in submission |
|---|---|---|
| Tech Implementation | On-device pose estimation (MediaPipe in a Kotlin native module) feeding a tested coaching core; Bedrock cue path with a latency fallback | Demo video, repo, architecture diagram |
| Design | Hero loop is clear without narration; usable with D-pad only; legible from across the room | Video shot from the user's position |
| Potential Impact | Credible user, market, and price story | Pitch slide using research §4–7 |
| Quality of Idea | Computer-vision fitness is explicitly listed as a "CREATIVE" example in the rules | Rules quote in the write-up |

**Measured in our own testing:**
- Fault detection agreement with manual labels on ≥ 3 recorded clips: ≥ 85% of frames.
- False cue rate: ≤ 1 spurious cue per 60 s of good-form plank.
- NFR-1 and NFR-3 met on the demo device.

## 11. Risks

| Risk | Likelihood | Impact | Mitigation |
|---|---|---|---|
| Vega OS doesn't expose raw camera frames to apps | Happened | Fatal for Vega | Built for Fire OS instead (Gate G1). Documented in FRICTION-LOG.md for the friction-log bonus |
| Pose pipeline too slow on the emulator (12 Oct: 72–150 ms inference but only 3–5 pose events/s at 4 vCPUs, software GPU) | High | High (NFR-1) | Profile the frame path (camera fps, bitmap rotation, bridge events) before tuning the model; MediaPipe GPU delegate if inference dominates |
| No camera-capable Fire TV hardware (cannot be ordered) | Certain | Med | Develop and demo on the laptop webcam (`/dev/video0`) through the simulator. Rules allow simulator/emulator demos (HACKATHON.md:74). Replay source keeps logic work unblocked |
| Bedrock latency makes cues feel late | Med | Med | Local pre-rendered cue fallback at 1.2 s (FR-13); pre-warm connection |
| Noisy landmarks cause spurious cues | Med | High (annoying) | Smoothing, debounce, cooldown, visibility gating (FR-3, FR-10, FR-12, FR-5) |
| Red/green skeleton unreadable for colour-blind users | Certain for some | Med | Colour + line style + icon encoding (BRANDING §4) |

**Rules note:** the research report advises filming on physical hardware "to avoid disqualification". The official rules say that a demo on the simulator or the Android TV Emulator is acceptable (HACKATHON.md:74), and the rules prevail. No camera-capable Fire TV hardware is available, so the demo runs on a simulator fed by the laptop webcam.
